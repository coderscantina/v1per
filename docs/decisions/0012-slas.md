# 0012: SLAs

Status: Adopted by Mike on 2026-10-23. Ships before the first public beta; milestone 12 now requires [milestone 14](../milestones/14-slas.md).

## Problem

Mike's client contracts promise availability, incident response and resolution times and response latency. V1Per measures uptime and runs incidents, but nothing states a target, tracks a period against it, warns before a breach or gives the client a record of what was met. Today that happens by hand outside the product.

## Constraints

- A client is a node with grants ([0001](0001-node-tree-and-access.md)). Clients must not learn about each other, and a client must not change their own contract.
- The client sees uptime on status pages. An SLA number for the same monitor and period must not differ from it.
- Rollups store up, down, pending and maintenance counts and latency min, max, sum and count. There are no latency percentiles. Imported Kuma history is mostly day rows, with minute rows for the last 24 hours only ([monitoring contract](../monitoring-contract.md#uptime)).
- Incidents have `started_at`, `resolved_at` and a timeline whose server-written updates have no author. There is no acknowledge action and no severity field.
- Billing is out of the first release.

## Choice

**The SLA**

- An SLA lives on a node and names monitors from that node's subtree, the same rule as status page components. A node can hold several SLAs.
- It has a name, a timezone, a period (month, quarter or year in that timezone) and a start date, the contract start.
- It holds up to four commitments, each optional: availability %, response time and resolution time per impact (`major_outage`, `partial_outage`, `degraded`), and average latency in milliseconds.
- Static components are not covered. An SLA covers monitors only.

**Availability**

- Measured 24/7 with the page uptime math: heartbeats, pending counts as down, maintenance excluded.
- Each SLA picks a combine mode:
  - `each`: the target applies to every covered monitor on its own. The SLA misses when any monitor misses.
  - `any_down`: one service timeline over minute buckets. A minute is down when any covered monitor has a down or pending beat in it.
- `any_down` reads a daily tally per SLA, written when a day closes in the SLA's timezone. Periods sum the tallies, so minute rollups need to outlive only the open day, not the period.

**Response and resolution**

- Counted incidents are all incidents, drafts included, that name a covered monitor. An incident belongs to the period it started in.
- Both clocks start at `started_at`. Response stops at the first update written by a person. Resolution stops at `resolved_at`.
- The target comes from the worst impact the incident reached on a covered monitor.
- Clocks count business time only: a weekly schedule and holiday dates stored on the SLA. Without a schedule they run 24/7. Maintenance does not pause them.
- A response is missed only when the incident is still open at its deadline without a human update. An incident that resolves first needs no response.

**Latency**

Average latency over up beats per covered monitor, from the monitor-level rollups. Exact for any period, imported history included. Only monitor types with latency count: http, keyword, json-query, port, ping, dns.

**Periods and history**

- Periods from the start date on are computed where the data allows, imported history included. A period the data cannot support shows "no data" for that commitment.
- A period is frozen as a stored result when it closes. Later changes to the SLA, its monitors or incidents never rewrite a frozen period. Staff can recompute one explicitly.

**Who sees and edits it**

- Reading needs Viewer on the SLA's node or an ancestor. Creating, editing and recomputing need Admin through a grant on a strict ancestor, or the instance admin. A client Admin on their own node reads only.
- A status page on a node can show SLAs from its subtree, opt in per SLA. It shows all commitments for the current period and the last 12 closed periods: targets, actuals, the share of incidents within target and average response and resolution times. It never shows incident titles or anything from drafts beyond these aggregates.
- The admin app has a printable report page per SLA and period, and a CSV of its per-monitor numbers and incidents.

**Alerts**

Through the notification outbox to channels picked on the SLA from its node or ancestors: a warning once per period when 75 % of the downtime budget is used, and a breach for missed availability, response deadline, resolution deadline and latency.

## Rejected alternatives

- A target field per monitor: no notion of one contract covering several services.
- SLA scope taken from a status page: ties contracts to what a page shows.
- Pending counted as up, or incident time counted as downtime: SLA numbers would differ from the page uptime the client sees.
- Average of monitors as a combine mode: one dead monitor hides behind healthy ones.
- p95 latency: needs a histogram in every rollup and has no imported history. Raw heartbeats cover 90 days only.
- An acknowledge action or a P1 to P4 severity field: new concepts where the first human update and the incident impact already carry the meaning.
- Business hours for availability: needs minute data for every period and makes the number differ from the page.
- Keeping minute rollups forever for `any_down`: about 23 GB a year at the target size ([m02](../evidence/m02-storage-gate.md)).
- Live recomputation of past periods: history shifts when a monitor is deleted or data ages out.
- Emailed reports and service credits: not wanted now; credits are billing.

## Consequences

- The minute rollup retention decision no longer depends on SLAs: the daily tally lets minute rollups drop to 90 days.
- The capability matrix gets an SLA row whose write rule is the first that looks at where a grant sits, not only the effective role.
- Status page reads gain an SLA section that must be tested per audience like incidents.
- The notification outbox gains SLA event kinds and templates in English and German.
- The beta waits for milestone 14.

## Affected contracts

Monitoring contract (SLA section), capability matrix, protocol SLA and page types, status page API, notification events, milestones 12 and 14.
